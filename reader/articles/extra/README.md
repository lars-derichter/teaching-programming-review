# Extra articles

These six papers were read in full as candidates for the reader and narrowly
missed the final selection of fifteen. Short introductions for students who
want to go deeper are in [section 10 of the reader](../../10-extra.md); the
table below records why each was set aside.

| File | Why it was set aside |
|------|----------------------|
| `porter-2013-peer-instruction.pdf` | Peer instruction halved fail rates across four courses; the mechanics and evidence are summarised in Brown & Wilson tip 2 (theme 1) and the theme 5 introduction. Excellent material for practising study critique (see its own threats-to-validity section). |
| `ben-ari-1998-constructivism.pdf` | Short, provocative primary source on constructivism in CS education ("the computer does not negotiate"). Its argument is covered in Sorva (2013), section 5. The PDF is a scan of the 1998 original. |
| `qian-lehman-2017-misconceptions.pdf` | Systematic catalogue of novice misconceptions and their sources; overlaps Sorva and Lister. Useful as a checklist when designing diagnostic questions. |
| `ericson-2022-parsons.pdf` | Working-group report on Parsons problems (equivalent learning at lower time cost). Very long; the headline is summarised in the theme 5 introduction. |
| `vihavainen-2014-systematic-review.pdf` | Systematic review: teaching interventions improve CS1 pass rates by roughly one third on average. The headline figure is cited in the reader introduction and theme 3. |
| `quille-bergin-2019-press.pdf` | The PreSS prediction model: programming self-efficacy as the dominant early predictor of CS1 success; prediction should trigger support, not selection. Summarised in the theme 7 introduction. |
